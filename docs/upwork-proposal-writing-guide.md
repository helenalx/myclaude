# Upwork Proposal Writing Guide

Strategies for writing high-converting Upwork proposals that stand out from generic, AI-generated submissions. Based on techniques shared by Michael Elliott.

## The 6 Steps

### 1. Conduct Deep Research First
Don't start writing until you've investigated the client's business.
- Review their website and understand their industry.
- Identify their main competitors.
- If you've worked with a competitor before, use that as your hook.

### 2. Lead with a Powerful Opening
You only get about three sentences of visible space before the client has to click "more." Use it to prove you read the brief:
- Mention the client's competitors.
- Offer a specific observation about a challenge in their project.
- Cite a relevant industry statistic.

### 3. Address the "Must-Haves" Directly
Explicitly acknowledge the specific skills or technical requirements listed in the job post (e.g., HubSpot/Salesforce attribution experience, years of experience). Keep these as quick, verifiable "ticks" — don't bury them in paragraphs.

### 4. Provide a Specific Opinion or Value Add
Skip the generic pitch. Give a professional opinion on a specific part of their brief (e.g., whether to keep or kill Performance Max campaigns). This demonstrates a unique perspective and real expertise, not a templated response.

### 5. Show, Don't Just Tell
Where possible, attach evidence of effort — a brief Loom video walkthrough or a short document with preliminary research or an audit. This is what separates you from the 40+ other freelancers who submitted no evidence at all.

### 6. Clear Call to Action
End by inviting the client to a call to diagnose the problem together. Emphasize your communication style — it's often a top concern for clients hiring freelancers.

## Pre-Submission Checklist

- [ ] Researched the client's website, industry, and competitors
- [ ] Opening 3 sentences reference something specific to this client (competitor, challenge, or stat)
- [ ] Every "must-have" requirement from the job post is explicitly addressed
- [ ] Included a specific, opinionated take on part of their brief
- [ ] Attached supporting evidence (Loom video, audit, or research doc) if possible
- [ ] Closed with a clear call to action for a call
- [ ] Reassured the client on communication/responsiveness
- [ ] Removed generic, copy-paste language that could apply to any client
